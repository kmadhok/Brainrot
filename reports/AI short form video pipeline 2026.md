# Automate the studio, not the slop

As of October 2026 the best way to rebuild your pipeline is as an **AI-native studio**: one to three channels, each with its own invented format or recurring character, where automation raises quality and output per hour of human editorial judgment. The old idea of a content farm that sprays near-identical Shorts across many channels no longer survives. The technical stack is mature and cheap enough that budget really is not a factor. Claude Opus 5.5 or GPT-6.1 Sol writes structured scripts. ElevenLabs Eleven v4 does voice, and its built-in timestamps replace the separate transcription step. ElevenLabs Music and SFX give you licensed audio. Google's Veo 3.1 and Gemini Omni Flash, plus MiniMax H3 and Kling 3.0 Omni via fal.ai, generate visuals. Remotion on Lambda renders word-level animated captions and the final edit. Temporal or Trigger.dev runs the workflow, and native publishing APIs feed the YouTube Analytics retention data back into scripting. All in, a premium 45-second Short costs roughly $10–25, and generative video is most of that. The binding constraint is now policy and economics, not production. YouTube's "inauthentic content" rule judges whole channels for templated sameness. In January 2026 it wiped out AI-slop channels with **about 4.72B combined views**. From **February 1, 2027**, Shorts revenue requires **10M qualified Shorts views in every trailing 90 days** ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220)). At third-party estimates of $0.03–$0.15 RPM, even 10M views earns only about $300–$1,500 per quarter. So Shorts should be treated as a distribution funnel into long-form, affiliate, product or brand revenue, not as the revenue source. Your early-2025 prototype is obsolete in nearly every component. Its LLM was in fact shut down a year before you wrote it. Its one durable idea, a hook-first script structure, is now a small part of what matters.

## Your 2025 prototype was dead on arrival, and the rest has aged out

The most important fact about the old code is that it could never have run as written. **OpenAI shut down `text-davinci-003` and the other InstructGPT completions models on January 4, 2024** ([OpenAI Deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations)), about a year before the early-2025 prototype. The repo has other faults too. `transcript.py` prints `OPENAI_API_KEY` after the line that loads it was commented out, so it would raise a `NameError` before any API call. `audio.py` ignores its `text` argument and always synthesizes the hard-coded sentence "All Gaul is divided into three parts" at an 8 kHz sample rate. `subtitles.py` transcribes an unrelated local MP3 instead of the Polly output. The three scripts were never wired together, and the stages that matter most were never built: video, assembly, orchestration, topic sourcing and publishing.

The other components fail on quality and architecture, not just because they are old. The 8 kHz choice was yours, not a limit of the service. Polly neural outputs up to 24 kHz ([AWS Polly docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/polly/latest/dg/generative-voices.html)). Still, no Polly engine appears in the top 20 of either the Artificial Analysis or Voice Arena blind-vote TTS leaderboards ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard); [Voice Arena](https://voicearena.com/)), and it has little emotion control, which expressive short-form narration needs. The AssemblyAI step is now redundant. When you control the script, ElevenLabs' `with-timestamps` endpoint returns character-level timings alongside the audio ([ElevenLabs API reference](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/api-reference/text-to-speech/convert-with-timestamps)). Re-transcribing your own TTS adds recognition errors to text you already have. SRT output also has the wrong granularity. It is line-level, while the dominant caption style pages 1–3 words at a time and highlights each word as it is spoken. LlamaIndex was overhead for a single prompt call. It only earns its place if you build a retrieval index over your own corpus, and hosted web-search tools from every major vendor have reduced even that need ([Anthropic Models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/overview)).

The prompt itself holds up better as an idea than as an implementation. Hook, then value, then CTA is still a reasonable skeleton. But an unstructured prompt that says "the content can be about any topic" and returns free text is close to the profile YouTube now demonetizes: generic output from one template with nothing traceable underneath. A 2026 script step produces structured JSON with timestamped beats, b-roll queries, on-screen text, a loop line and sourced `claims[]`. It also checks each draft against the channel's recent catalog so the scripts don't converge.

| 2025 component | October 2026 status | Replacement | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `text-davinci-003` via LlamaIndex | Shut down Jan 4, 2024 | Claude Opus 5.5 / GPT-6.1 Sol via direct SDK, structured outputs | Model gone; framework unnecessary for a linear DAG |
| Single "any topic" prompt | Conceptually dated | Multi-step: evidence pack → hook tournament → beat outline → JSON script → critique → verify → originality check | Templated generic output is the demonetized profile |
| AWS Polly neural, 8 kHz MP3 | Uncompetitive | ElevenLabs Eleven v4 (Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS as A/B) | Outside top 20 on both arenas; weak emotion control |
| AssemblyAI SRT | Redundant | TTS timestamps or forced alignment against the known script | No transcription errors; word-level timing |
| (Not built) visuals, assembly, orchestration, publishing, analytics | — | Generative video + Remotion + durable workflow + native APIs + retention loop | Now the cheapest, most commoditized parts |

The irony is that the parts you never built have become the easy parts. The hard part, a format that platforms will keep paying for, was never in the prototype at all.

## Policy and economics now decide the architecture

### YouTube judges channels, not videos, and templates are the target

YouTube does not ban AI. Its monetization policy disallows "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates", "image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value", stitched "unrelated or inconsistent AI clips to surprise or shock viewers", and storylines where characters "face the same situation with the same outcome". It **explicitly allows** using AI "to visualize a unique character and narrative you invented", to edit scripts, or to generate a unique background ([YouTube Help: channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)). The reused-content section is just as pointed for brainrot formats. It disallows "content that exclusively features readings of other materials you did not originally create", which is a close description of TTS narration of a scraped Reddit post ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)). The policy was renamed from "repetitious" to "inauthentic content" on July 15, 2025, and it applies to "the channel as a whole" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)). In July 2026 YouTube clarified three demonetizable categories: generic content that is easy to produce with AI or templates, off-putting content made to emotionally manipulate, and AI personas standing in for the creator, which YouTube especially doesn't want on finance, legal or medical topics ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/); [Stuff](https://stuff.co.za/2026/07/21/youtube-cracks-down-on-ai-slop-with-new-revenue/)).

Enforcement is real and aims at the biggest wins. In his January 2026 letter, CEO Neal Mohan made reducing "AI slop" a priority ([Variety](https://au.variety.com/2026/digital/news/youtube-channels-using-ai-tools-reduce-slop-neal-mohan-letter-32190/)). Within weeks YouTube **terminated 11 of the most popular AI channels and wiped all videos from 6 more**. Together they had roughly 35M subscribers and 4.72B views ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/entertainment/youtube-responds-after-deleting-popular-ai-slop-channels-with-millions-of-subscribers-3314829/)). Outlets disagree on the exact split, and some report "16 channels". TNW estimates the removed channels earned about $10M a year. It also reports collateral damage: human-made faceless channels were demonetized too, the algorithm appears to favor on-camera hosts, and since March 2026 viewers get a pop-up asking whether a video "feels like AI slop" ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)). Mechanics such as a "last 30 uploads" window or a 90-day suspension ladder come only from secondary sources. No platform publishes a safe posting cadence. The lesson is that **reach and termination risk go together**. The formats that grew fastest, such as AI animal stories, AI fairy tales and quote narration ([Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/blog/ai-slop-report-the-global-rise-of-low-quality-ai-videos/)), are the ones that match the policy's own examples.

### The February 2027 Shorts floor turns Shorts into marketing

The economics were already thin. Shorts ad revenue is pooled, and creators keep 45% of their engaged-view share ([YouTube Help: Shorts monetization](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220)). Third-party RPM estimates run $0.03–$0.15 per 1,000 views ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-monetization/)), while long-form earns $2–$15+ ([Korpi](https://korpi.ai/blog/how-much-do-faceless-youtube-channels-make)). The official Help page now adds a hard gate. From **February 1, 2027**, a channel must have **10M qualified Shorts views in the trailing 90 days** to earn Shorts ad and Premium revenue for a month. Channels below the floor stay in YPP and keep their long-form earnings ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-cuts-shorts-pay-for-channels-under-10m-views-from-february-2027/)). That is about 3.3M views a month just to earn anything. Below the line, Shorts pay is zero. At the line, it is pocket change. A mass-automation operator might try to clear the floor by consolidating volume into one channel. That concentrates the same templated catalog in exactly the place channel-level enforcement looks, so the two policies reinforce each other against the farm model.

### TikTok and Meta reward originality and length differently

TikTok's Creator Rewards pays **only for original videos over one minute**. Its formula weights originality, play duration, search value and engagement ([RouteNote](https://routenote.com/blog/tiktok-launch-creator-rewards-program/); [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/phones/tiktok-launches-new-creator-rewards-program-to-pay-creators-who-make-trending-search-content)). Creators report about $0.40–$1.00 per 1,000 qualified views; TikTok publishes no rate ([TokConnect](https://tokconnect.com/guides/tiktok-creator-rewards-program/)). Since January 22, 2026, US TikTok has been run by the TikTok USDS Joint Venture, and creators report volatile reach and RPM since then ([Thothium](https://thothium.com/blog/tiktok-oracle-algorithm-rebuild-2026)). That volatility is a risk to any plan that depends on TikTok. In April 2026 Instagram stopped recommending accounts that mostly share content they didn't create or meaningfully transform. Adam Mosseri pointed instead to remixing, adding your own commentary or voiceover, native reposts, or collaborating with the original creator ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/)). Facebook demotes duplicates and says watermarks or stitched clips are not "meaningful enhancements" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/following-youtube-meta-announces-crackdown-on-unoriginal-facebook-content)). The practical result is that **one master edit can't serve every platform**. You need a 60–90-second cut for TikTok monetization and shorter cuts for Shorts and Reels reach. No authoritative optimal length exists, and vendor benchmarks contradict each other ([Shno](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/short-form-video-statistics); [Vidico](https://vidico.com/news/short-form-video-statistics/)).

### Labeling is mandatory, cheap, and not a shield

YouTube requires disclosure of realistic synthetic scenes, altered real events, and real people shown saying or doing things they didn't. It does not require disclosure for fantastical or fully animated content, for scripting, captions or thumbnails, or for cloning your own voice. It states that "disclosure itself doesn't limit a video's audience or affect monetization eligibility" ([YouTube Help: disclosing synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)). Labels triggered by C2PA metadata or YouTube's own tools are permanent ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/05/27/youtube-generative-ai-labels-easier-to-read-apply/)). TikTok reads C2PA, runs detection models, and is piloting invisible watermarks designed to survive re-uploads ([TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/tiktok-ssa-shares-more-ways-to-spot-shape-and-understand-ai-generated-content?lang=en-ZA)). In the EU, **AI Act Article 50 transparency duties have applied since August 2, 2026**. Under the Code of Practice, deployers must label deepfakes at first exposure and must not remove provider watermarks ([Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content)). New York's synthetic-performer ad disclosure law took effect June 9, 2026 ([Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/new-york-synthetic-performer-law-what-advertisers-need-to-know)), and the FTC's fake-review rule covers AI testimonials ([Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2024/08/30/u-s-ftcs-new-rule-on-fake-and-ai-generated-reviews-and-social-media-bots/)). The pipeline rule is simple. Set `containsSyntheticMedia` on YouTube and `is_aigc` on TikTok whenever a realistic scene is generated, never strip C2PA, and don't treat labels as protection: disclosure does not exempt templated content.

Rights add two more constraints. Tennessee's ELVIS Act makes unauthorized voice replicas actionable, including against tools ([Armstrong Teasdale](https://www.armstrongteasdale.com/thought-leadership/artificial-intelligence-and-copyrights-tennessees-elvis-act-becomes-law/)). The federal NO FAKES Act cleared Senate Judiciary in June 2026 but is not law ([Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/4591)). Clone only a contracted voice with written AI-use consent. On music, **UMG and Sony sued Suno a second time on September 18, 2026**, targeting the new v6 model, even as Warner, BMG and Believe partnered with Suno ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/universal-and-sony-sue-suno-for-a-second-time-claiming-platforms-v6-models-are-the-fruit-of-the-same-poisoned-tree/)). Udio is reportedly becoming a walled garden with no exports ([replayedstudio](https://replayedstudio.com/blog/suno-warner-udio-universal-deals/)). Neither is a safe default for monetized output.

### What actually performs is the weakest evidence in the field

Independent, platform-verified retention data barely exists, and the vendor benchmarks contradict each other. The well-documented facts point both ways. Slop gets reach: in Kapwing's test, 21% of the first 500 Shorts served to a new account were AI-generated ([Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/blog/ai-slop-report-the-global-rise-of-low-quality-ai-videos/)). Trust is falling: 49% of US consumers say GenAI has made content worse ([CX Today/Gartner](https://www.cxtoday.com/marketing-sales-technology/gartner-ai-generated-content-quality-marketing-fatigue/)), and 36% of people who spotted an AI video said it lowered their trust in the brand ([Animoto via BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260121875037/en/83-of-Consumers-Can-Spot-AI-Videos-36-Say-It-Lowers-Brand-Trust-According-to-Animotos-New-Report)). Supply is effectively unlimited; Higgsfield alone generates about 4.5M videos a day ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)). The strongest case for mass automation is that it still works for reach, and a disposable-channel operator could treat terminations as a cost of doing business. That model now fails on the money: Shorts pay requires concentrated scale, TikTok pays only for long originals, and Meta withholds non-follower distribution from unoriginal accounts. What survives, in the policy text and in reported survivor behavior, is **a real point of view, original research or invented narrative, distinctive editing, and fewer, better uploads** ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026/); [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)). Some faceless operators are even hiring on-camera hosts ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)).

### Ideation: own the ideas and use trends only as signals

No platform offers an open trending API. Google's official Trends API, announced July 2025, is still an application-only alpha with no published pricing ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-opens-alpha-testing-for-new-trends-api-targeting-developers-and-journalists/)), and `pytrends` is described as dead ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/esteban_ortega/pytrends-is-dead-heres-how-to-get-google-trends-data-in-2026-1a18)). TikTok Creative Center has no public API, and the Research API is restricted to academics ([EchoTik](https://www.echotik.live/blog/tiktok-research-api-free-access-guide/)). Reddit's free API is non-commercial only. Commercial access requires a contract, and its 2026 pricing is unconfirmed ([SocialCrawl](https://www.socialcrawl.dev/blog/best-reddit-data-apis-2026)). Apply for the Trends alpha, use YouTube-native signals, and buy licensed data where it exists. Treat Apify-style scrapers as a legal exposure that falls on you, not the vendor. More fundamentally, the trend layer should feed angles into **original** scripts, never source text, because reading other people's material is the clearest policy violation. The ideation stage also owns a new compliance job: scoring each candidate's similarity to the channel's last N videos, and rejecting ideas that would make the catalog look templated.

### Scripting: structured, grounded, and judged against real retention

The current frontier lineup is Anthropic's Opus 5.5 ($4/$20 per MTok), Sonnet 5.5, Haiku 5.5 and Fable 5.1 ([Anthropic Models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/overview)); OpenAI's GPT-6 Sol/Luna and the DevDay GPT-6.1 Sol ([Unite.ai](https://www.unite.ai/openai-unveils-gpt-6.1-sol-at-devday-with-new-codex-and-chatgpt-tools/)); and Google's Gemini 3.8 Flash ([Gemini API models](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models)). No credible head-to-head benchmark on short-form scriptwriting exists, so "best model" is a judgment call. Use Opus 5.5 or GPT-6.1 Sol to draft, and a model from another vendor to judge and fact-check, which reduces self-preference bias. The pipeline is a deterministic sequence. A research step uses first-party web search to build an evidence pack. A hook generator produces many candidates, and a judge ranks them in a pairwise tournament. The model then writes a timestamped beat outline, followed by a JSON script with `claims[]` that each cite an evidence ID. A separate verifier deletes any unsupported claim rather than softening it. Caching the channel "bible" costs 5% of base input on Opus/Sonnet 5.5, and the Batch API is 50% off ([Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/overview)), so nightly bulk drafting costs almost nothing. One caution: **no published evidence shows that LLM hook scores predict real retention**. The LLM-as-judge literature stresses calibration against humans ([arXiv 2411.15594](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.15594)), and VideoJudge found that multimodal judges watching the rendered video beat text-only judges ([arXiv 2509.21451](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21451v1)). Treat the judge as a hypothesis until your own analytics confirm it.

### Voice, music and captions: one timeline drives everything

ElevenLabs Eleven v4 and v4 Turbo launched September 28, 2026. They have inline emotion and SFX tags and 10-second instant cloning ([Unite.AI](https://unite.ai/elevenlabs-launches-eleven-v4-with-low-latency-turbo-variant)), and v4 costs $0.08 per 1K characters ([ElevenLabs pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api)). The arenas disagree on the top spot. Artificial Analysis ranks v4 Turbo and v4 first and second, while Voice Arena puts Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS first with overlapping confidence intervals ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard); [Voice Arena](https://voicearena.com/)). So run a blind A/B on your own scripts between v4 and Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS, which costs $16.5 per 1M characters. Turbo matters only for live use. Use a distinctive house voice, cloned from a contracted voice actor with written consent, instead of saturated library or TikTok-style voices. That voice is a brand asset and a signal against the "generic" label. If you self-host, Kokoro (Apache 2.0) and Chatterbox (MIT) are commercially safe. F5-TTS official weights, Breeze and Fish S2 are not ([codesota](https://www.codesota.com/guides/tts-models)). For music beds, ElevenLabs Music was licensed from Merlin and Kobalt before training ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/ai-story/elevenlabs-eleven-music-ai-generator-launch)), and ElevenLabs SFX is royalty-free ([ElevenLabs pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api)). Keep generation logs as license evidence. Note also that Shorts using tracks from YouTube's music program divert half or two-thirds of their pool share to rights holders ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220)). Captions follow from the timestamps. Group ElevenLabs' character timings into words, map them back to the script's spelling, and render with Remotion's `createTikTokStyleCaptions()`, which pages tokens and supports active-word highlighting ([Remotion docs](https://remotion.dev/docs/captions/create-tiktok-style-captions)). For any other TTS, run ElevenLabs Forced Alignment at about $0.22 per audio hour ([fal.ai](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/elevenlabs/forced-alignment/llms.txt)). ASR becomes a QA step only: transcribe the rendered audio and diff it against the script to catch skipped or misread words.

### Visuals: keyframes first, then image-to-video, never borrowed gameplay

The leaderboards changed sharply this year. **OpenAI shut down the Sora 2 API on September 24, 2026, with no replacement** ([OpenAI Deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations)), and the Sora app closed in April ([Engadget](https://engadget.com/ai/openai-is-shutting-down-its-sora-video-generation-app-211023358.html)). Artificial Analysis now ranks Wan 3.0 first in text-to-video ($12/min of 1080p), followed by Seedance 2.5, MiniMax H3 and FLUX 3. In image-to-video, **MiniMax H3 Max leads at about $4.80/min**, with Gemini Omni Flash close behind. Runway, Luma and Pika are absent from the top 15 ([AA T2V](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/text-to-video); [AA I2V](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/image-to-video)). Google now recommends Gemini Omni Flash as its default video model, including multi-turn conversational editing. Veo 3.1 is kept for native audio, extension and first/last-frame control, at $0.40/s Standard down to $0.05/s Lite ([Gemini API video docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/video); [Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). Make Google's first-party API your primary vendor for its SLAs and editing. Route MiniMax H3, Kling 3.0 Omni (15-second multi-shot storyboards with character references) and Wan through fal.ai so you can swap models as rankings move ([fal.ai](https://fal.ai/learn/tools/best-text-to-video-apis-2026)). Seedance 2.5 offers 30-second clips and 30 image references, but is reportedly unavailable in the US through BytePlus ([leadde](https://leadde.ai/blog/how-much-is-seedance-2-5)).

The reliable workflow is to generate keyframes, then animate them. Create hero stills with GPT Image 2.5 or Nano Banana Pro. Migrate now, because `gpt-image-1` retires October 23, 2026 ([OpenAI Deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations)). For a recurring character that stays pixel-stable across hundreds of videos, train a FLUX.2 LoRA ([techstackups](https://techstackups.com/comparisons/gemini-vs-openai-vs-flux-vs-runway-character-consistency-may-2026/)). Then animate the stills with I2V. That recurring invented character is the strongest policy asset available, since YouTube explicitly permits AI that visualizes "a unique character and narrative you invented". Drop the classic split-screen gameplay background. Downloaded Subway Surfers or parkour footage is third-party reuse, and a fixed "voiceover over gameplay" layout is a near-perfect "template with little variation" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)). Generated per-video backgrounds or self-recorded footage avoid both problems. Pexels is still free for real-world establishing shots ([Pexels API](https://www.pexels.com/api/documentation/)). HeyGen Avatar V ($0.10/s via fal, 9:16) can serve as a recurring presenter ([fal.ai](https://fal.ai/learn/tools/best-text-to-video-apis-2026)), but an AI host covering finance, health or law is one of the categories YouTube named as non-monetizable.

### Assembly, orchestration and QA: declarative edits inside a durable workflow

Remotion (~63k stars) is the default programmatic compositor. Automated rendering needs the "Automators" license, which costs $0.01 per render with a $100/month minimum ([Remotion pricing](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/pricing); [License FAQ](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/license/faq)). Lambda's default concurrency of 1,000 per region far exceeds any sane Shorts volume ([Remotion Lambda limits](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/lambda/limits)). The "Remotion Skills" package lets Claude Code write Remotion compositions directly ([Timewell](https://timewell.jp/en/columns/remotion-claude-code-programmatic-video)), which is useful for designing a family of distinct templates. The right contract is a typed **edit decision list**: a JSON file of scenes, assets, word timings and caption style that an LLM emits, a validator checks, Remotion renders, and ffmpeg normalizes and transcodes. MoviePy 2.x, used by most open-source "money printer" repos, broke its 1.x API and has had no release since May 2025 ([GitHub](https://github.com/Zulko/moviepy); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/moviepy)). MoneyPrinterTurbo (~130k stars) is a useful reference implementation ([GitHub](https://github.com/harry0703/MoneyPrinterTurbo)). Its stock-clip-plus-TTS output, though, is exactly the templated look that gets channels demonetized.

For orchestration, put a durable-execution engine underneath and agents inside individual steps. An agent should not free-run the whole pipeline. Temporal suits a Python-heavy stack; Trigger.dev or Inngest suit a TypeScript/Remotion stack ([Noqta](https://noqta.tn/blog/durable-execution-ai-agents-inngest-trigger-temporal-2026)). Keep media in object storage, not workflow history. Use the Claude Agent SDK or OpenAI Agents SDK only for open-ended steps: research, fact verification and the weekly analytics review. Approval gates belong after the script and storyboard, where changes are cheap, and after render, before publish. Wire them as workflow signals from Slack buttons. The QA gate combines deterministic checks (ffprobe for 1080×1920, duration and loudness), an ASR-versus-script diff, a multimodal model watching the render against a rubric (hook in the first two seconds, caption safe zones, generation artifacts, brand safety), and a catalog-level similarity check. Keep a per-video cost ledger, because observability tools such as Langfuse track LLM tokens but not video-generation seconds. No published case study validates vision-LLM QA on rendered Shorts, so calibrate it against human review the same way as the script judge.

### Publishing and analytics: the native APIs got easier, and the gates moved

YouTube cut the quota cost of an upload from about 1,600 to **about 100 units on December 4, 2025**, and moved `videos.insert` into its own quota bucket on June 1, 2026 ([YouTube API revision history](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/revision_history)), so quota no longer limits a few channels. TikTok's Content Posting API forces **all posts to private until your app passes an audit**. It also enforces 6 requests per minute and unpublished daily caps. `is_aigc=true` applies the AI label, and draft (inbox) mode is a natural human-review step ([TikTok Direct Post reference](https://developers.tiktok.com/docs/en/content-posting-api-reference-direct-post)). Instagram Reels publish through a container-then-publish flow. Meta's own docs disagree on whether the cap is 50 or 100 posts per 24 hours, so query `content_publishing_limit` at runtime. Trial Reels shown only to non-followers act as a free A/B gate ([Meta content publishing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/content-publishing)). Ayrshare, from $149/month, lets you launch on its audited TikTok app while your own audit is pending ([Ayrshare](https://www.ayrshare.com/pricing.md)). For the feedback loop, the YouTube Analytics API exposes `audienceWatchRatio` and `relativeRetentionPerformance` by `elapsedVideoTimeRatio`, plus `engagedViews` ([YouTube Analytics metrics](https://developers.google.com/youtube/analytics/metrics)). Align each drop-off and spike with the stored beat timestamps. A watch ratio above 1 near the end means the loop worked. Feed the best and worst hooks back as few-shot examples, and use the same labels to re-weight the judge. I could not confirm whether the Shorts "viewed vs swiped away" metric is exposed through the API.

## Recommended architecture and phased build plan

The architecture is a durable workflow per video, with a second, slower loop per channel that learns from analytics. Every arrow below is a typed artifact stored in object storage and logged with its cost.

      ▼
[Ideation]  Haiku 5.5: cluster → dedupe → catalog-similarity + policy-risk score ──► Human picks angles (weekly)
      │
      ▼

      │
      ▼

Two disciplines make the plan survive enforcement. First, **set a cadence ceiling and a variety floor before launch**. Cap uploads at what your editors can genuinely approve. Make the catalog-similarity check a hard block, not a warning, because enforcement looks at the channel's pattern. Second, **define kill criteria**. If a format cannot earn non-follower reach on Instagram Trial Reels or beat a `relativeRetentionPerformance` of 0.5 after a set number of uploads, retire it rather than scale it. Because the enforcement mechanics are opaque, assume human review can see your whole catalog, and build only what you would defend line by line.

## Conclusion

In eighteen months the problem has turned upside down. In early 2025 the hard part looked like production: getting an LLM, a voice and subtitles to cooperate. By October 2026 production is a commodity costing roughly $10–25 per premium Short, and the scarce inputs are platform permission and audience trust. That changes what automation is for. Its job is no longer to multiply output. It is to keep a distinctive format consistent, keep every claim sourced, keep the catalog varied, and close the loop between retention curves and the next script. Those are exactly the properties the policies reward. The modules most worth investing in are not the generators, which churn monthly and sit behind swappable aggregator calls. They are the ones no vendor sells: the originality check against your own catalog, the claim verifier, and the judge calibrated against your own retention data.

The February 2027 floor is a strategic signal more than a revenue line. YouTube is choosing to pay concentrated, proven channels and treat the long tail of Shorts as free promotion. An operator who accepts that early, and uses Shorts to build an owned character, audience and long-form or commercial channel, is building something that gains value as slop floods the feed. An operator who fights it with volume is building the kind of catalog the January 2026 terminations removed. Much of the 2026 evidence base is vendor-sourced, platform mechanics are deliberately opaque, and model rankings will have moved again within months. Treat the specific vendor picks as current defaults and expect to replace them. The underlying strategy does not depend on any particular vendor.
