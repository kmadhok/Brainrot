# Platform Policy, Monetization & What Performs: Faceless AI Short-Form Video (as of October 2026)

Scope: YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Instagram/Facebook Reels; US and EU. Research date: 2026-10-11. Source quality warning: much 2026 coverage of this topic is SEO and vendor blogs, often from companies selling AI video tools. I used official help pages and mainstream or trade press where I could, and flagged where I couldn't. Snapchat Spotlight was not researched; no findings.

## 1. YouTube: YPP "inauthentic content" policy, reused content, Shorts revenue sharing, and how AI faceless channels have fared

### Takeaway
YouTube does not ban AI. It demonetizes, and in the worst cases terminates, channels whose output is templated, mass-produced or repetitive. The policy was renamed from "repetitious" to "inauthentic content" on July 15, 2025. Enforcement escalated in January 2026, when 16 of the largest AI-slop channels were terminated or wiped. Separately, from Feb 1, 2027, a channel must reach 10M qualified Shorts views in every rolling 90 days to keep earning Shorts revenue. That makes small Shorts-only automation channels much harder to monetize.

### Cited Findings
**Policy text (official YouTube Help, "YouTube channel monetization policies")**
- July 15, 2025: the "repetitious content" policy was renamed "inauthentic content," clarifying that it covers "content that is repetitive or mass-produced." The body text still uses the heading "Generic or Repetitive Content." — [YouTube Help: channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Not allowed, quoted from the page: "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value"; "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates"; stitching "unrelated or inconsistent AI clips to surprise or shock viewers"; videos where characters face the same situation with the same outcome (a highly similar storyline template). — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Allowed, per the same page: using AI to visualize a unique character and narrative you invented; using AI to edit scripts or generate a unique background visual; a shared intro and outro when the bulk of each video differs. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Reused content: content is acceptable if "viewers can tell that there's a meaningful difference between the original video and your video." Not allowed: content copied "without any substantive modifications"; "Content that exclusively features readings of other materials you did not originally create" (e.g., text from websites or news feeds); pitch- or speed-shifted songs. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
  - Pipeline implication: a TTS reading of a Reddit post you did not write fits the "readings of other materials you did not originally create" example almost exactly.
- The policy page does not explicitly mention text-to-speech, and it has no standalone numeric "mass production" rule (no posting cap). — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)

**Enforcement and outcomes**
- In his January 21, 2026 annual letter, CEO Neal Mohan called reducing "AI slop" a 2026 priority: "To reduce the spread of low quality AI content, we're actively building on our established systems that have been very successful in combatting spam and clickbait." He also said that in December 2025 more than 1M channels a day used YouTube's own AI creation tools, and that creators will be able to make Shorts with their own likeness. — [Variety](https://au.variety.com/2026/digital/news/youtube-channels-using-ai-tools-reduce-slop-neal-mohan-letter-32190/); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/01/21/neal-mohans-2026-letter-to-creators-a-lot-of-ai-a-tiktok-shop-competitor-and-beautifully-produced-tv-to-compete-with-netflix/)
- Late January 2026: YouTube banned 11 of the most popular AI-content channels and wiped all videos from 6 more. Per Kapwing data, they had about 4.72B combined views and 35M subscribers. YouTube's statement: "YouTube doesn't allow spam, scams, or other deceptive practices that take advantage of the YouTube community." — [Dexerto, Feb 5 2026](https://www.dexerto.com/entertainment/youtube-responds-after-deleting-popular-ai-slop-channels-with-millions-of-subscribers-3314829/)
  - Conflict: TNW and others describe this as "16 channels terminated." Dexerto's 11 terminated plus 6 wiped adds to 17. The exact split is inconsistent across outlets. — [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)
- Estimated income of the removed channels was about $10M/year. Enforcement is reported to work at the channel level, where a pattern across recent uploads can demonetize the whole channel. TNW reports a "last 30 uploads" window, which I could not confirm in official docs. — [TNW, June 15 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)
- March 2026: YouTube began a mobile pop-up asking viewers to rate whether a video "feels like AI slop" on a five-point scale. YouTube has not said how ratings are weighted or whether they affect monetization or reach. — [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)
- Collateral damage: TNW reports the algorithm appears to favor on-camera hosts, and that human-made faceless channels say they are being demonetized too. Doctor NOS (1.7M subscribers): "the people who do the same content as me without their face in it, most of them are getting demonetised." Some creators are hiring cheap on-camera hosts via Fiverr or Upwork (per The Hollywood Reporter, cited by TNW). — [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)
- Penalty ladder, from secondary sources only (not verified on official pages): a 90-day monetization suspension for unaddressed violations, then permanent YPP removal. Rene Ritchie (YouTube Head of Creator Liaison) reportedly said YouTube "welcomes creators using AI tools to enhance storytelling." — [OutlierKit (vendor blog)](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026/)
- Pressure on kids' content: a New York Times investigation found more than 40% of Shorts recommended after popular preschool videos contained low-quality AI content. In April 2026, a coalition of 230 experts sent an open letter asking YouTube to ban AI content from YouTube Kids. — [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)

**Shorts revenue sharing (official)**
- Revenue from ads between Shorts is pooled monthly. Shorts without music send all revenue to the Creator Pool. Shorts with one track send half; two tracks send one-third. Creators are allocated pool revenue by their share of engaged views per country and keep 45%, whether or not they used music. — [YouTube Help: Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220)
- Views that don't count: non-original content, such as unedited movie or TV clips, reuploads, or "compilations without added original content"; bot views; views on content that isn't advertiser-friendly. Shorts over one minute that contain claimed content are not monetizable. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220)
- **New, effective Feb 1, 2027:** a channel must have 10M qualified Shorts views in the trailing 90 days (measured to the 15th of the preceding month) to earn Shorts ad and Premium revenue for that month. Separately, Shorts ads targeted to groups of five or fewer channels will pay direct revenue at a 45% creator share. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220)
  - Announced around Aug 10, 2026. Channels below the floor stay in YPP and keep long-form earnings, and Shorts pay resumes automatically if they cross the threshold again. Press also reports higher entry thresholds for new applicants, with figures cited as 8,000 watch hours or 20M Shorts views in 90 days, though outlets are unclear how these relate. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-cuts-shorts-pay-for-channels-under-10m-views-from-february-2027/); [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/industry-briefing-news/youtube-sets-10-million-shorts-views-for-monetisation-from-2027-157245.html); [Cartoon Brew](https://cartoonbrew.com/shorts/youtube-shorts-monetization-rule-changes-265682.html)
- Shorts RPM, third-party estimates only: about $0.03–$0.15 per 1,000 views, with US at the higher end. YouTube publishes no official figure. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-monetization/); [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/youtube-shorts-monetization/)

**Scale of slop (Kapwing, Nov/Dec 2025; now about 10 months old)**
- Of the first 500 Shorts served to a fresh account, 21% (104 videos) were AI-generated and 33% were "brainrot." The top 10 AI-slop channels earned an estimated $33.6M/year, a Social Blade-based estimate rather than reported income. The top earner, India's Bandar Apna Dost, had about 2.07B views and an estimated $4.25M/year. The most-subscribed was the Spanish-language "Cuentos Facinantes," with about 5.95M subscribers. — [Kapwing AI Slop Report](https://www.kapwing.com/blog/ai-slop-report-the-global-rise-of-low-quality-ai-videos/); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/one-in-five-youtube-shorts-shown-to-new-users-is-ai-generated-slop-study-finds/)

### Inferences
- The January 2026 removals hit the largest, most obviously templated channels. That suggests enforcement goes after the channel-level pattern of identical formats, not single videos. For an automated pipeline, the main risk is that format sameness across the whole catalog gets flagged, more than any single video.
- The Feb 2027 floor of 10M qualified views every 90 days (about 3.3M a month) means a new automated Shorts-only channel may earn $0 in Shorts revenue for a long time. Shorts should be treated mainly as a funnel to long-form, affiliate, or brand revenue rather than a direct revenue source. At $0.03–$0.15 RPM, 10M views is only about $300–$1,500 per 90 days anyway.
- TTS readings of other people's text (Reddit posts, news articles) are the format most directly described by the reused-content examples.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube statement gives a numeric upload-frequency limit, or confirms the "last 30 uploads" or "90-day suspension" mechanics. These come from secondary sources.
- I found no official figures on how many channels were demonetized under the inauthentic-content policy overall. Claims of "thousands" are unverified.
- Whether the "AI slop" viewer survey affects ranking is undisclosed.

## 2. AI labeling and disclosure: platform rules and legal requirements (EU AI Act, US states, FTC)

### Takeaway
All three major platforms now auto-label AI content using C2PA metadata, their own tools' watermarks, and classifiers. YouTube and TikTok require creators to self-disclose realistic synthetic content. Disclosure itself is not penalized in reach or monetization on YouTube, but it does not protect templated content. Legally, the EU AI Act Article 50 transparency duties have applied since Aug 2, 2026, with a grace period to Dec 2, 2026 for some provider marking duties. California's AI Transparency Act took effect Aug 2, 2026, and New York's synthetic-performer ad disclosure law took effect June 9, 2026.

### Cited Findings
**YouTube**
- Disclosure is required for realistic AI or altered content that makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't, alters footage of real events or places, generates a realistic scene that didn't occur, or creates music that is the main focus. It applies to fully or partially AI content. — [YouTube Help: Disclosing altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- Disclosure is not required for non-realistic or fantastical content, fully animated AI animation, minor aesthetic edits, or production help (scripts, thumbnails, titles, captions, upscaling, idea generation). Cloning your own voice for voiceovers is also exempt. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- "Disclosure itself doesn't limit a video's audience or affect monetization eligibility." Creators who repeatedly fail to disclose may get labels applied, have content removed, or be suspended from YPP. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- YouTube may auto-label content made with its GenAI tools, content carrying C2PA metadata, or content its systems detect. Labels from YouTube tools, C2PA, or manual review cannot be changed by the creator. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- TNW reports YouTube also uses SynthID watermarks, and that labels are permanent for content made with YouTube's own tools (Veo, Gemini Omni). The official help page does not mention SynthID. — [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)
- Disclosure and the inauthentic-content rule are separate: disclosure does not exempt templated content. — [LensPOV (blog)](https://lenspov.com/articles/youtube-ai-content-demonetization-2026)

**TikTok**
- TikTok labels AI content through creator labeling tools, its own detection models, and C2PA Content Credentials, and has labeled more than 1.3B videos. It is piloting invisible watermarks, readable only by TikTok, on AI content made with TikTok tools (e.g., AI Editor Pro) and on uploads carrying C2PA. The watermark is meant to survive re-uploads and edits. An AIGC feed setting lets users adjust how much AI content they see. — [TikTok Newsroom, Nov 2025](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/more-ways-to-spot-shape-and-understand-ai-content?lang=en); [TikTok Newsroom, Mar 10 2026](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/tiktok-ssa-shares-more-ways-to-spot-shape-and-understand-ai-generated-content?lang=en-ZA)
- TikTok requires labels for AI content containing realistic images, audio or video, and encourages labels for fully AI or significantly edited content. — [Storrito (secondary)](https://storrito.com/resources/what-tiktoks-ai-monetization-restrictions-signal-for-creator-income/)

**Meta (Instagram/Facebook)**
- Meta replaced its "Made with AI" label with "AI info" in July 2024 after false-positive complaints. Detection reads C2PA and IPTC metadata. — [Jang (news)](https://jang.com.pk/en/15206-meta-replaces-made-with-ai-label-with-ai-info-amid-user-criticism-news); [makeinfluence (secondary)](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/content-credentials-c2pa-how-platforms-label-ai-assisted-content)
- Unverified secondary claims: in July 2026 Meta auto-enforced "AI info" on ads made with Meta tools or carrying C2PA from third-party tools. Separately, labeled organic posts were reportedly not penalized in reach as of early 2026. — [billo.app (vendor)](https://billo.app/blog/ai-labeling/); [Coinis (vendor)](https://coinis.com/blog/meta-ai-content-labeling-facebook-instagram-ads-2026)

**EU AI Act**
- Article 50 transparency obligations, including deepfake labeling by deployers, apply from Aug 2, 2026. The Digital Omnibus on AI (reached political agreement May 7, 2026; in force July 27, 2026) delayed high-risk obligations but not Article 50. — [Legalithm](https://www.legalithm.com/en/blog/eu-ai-act-what-applies-2-august-2026); [artificialintelligenceact.eu](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/)
- The Omnibus gives providers of generative systems already on the market until Dec 2, 2026 to meet the Article 50(2) machine-readable marking duty. Deepfakes created before Aug 2, 2026 carry no retroactive labeling duty. — [Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content); [Legalithm](https://www.legalithm.com/en/blog/eu-ai-act-what-applies-2-august-2026)
- The Commission adopted final Article 50 guidelines on July 20, 2026 and assessed the Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content as adequate. The Code has a provider section (marking and detection, 50(2)) and a deployer section (labeling, 50(4)). Deployers must label deepfakes at first exposure and must not remove provider watermarks. — [Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content); [Jones Day on draft Code, Jan 2026](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/01/european-commission-publishes-draft-code-of-practice-on-ai-labelling-and-transparency); [TechPolicy.Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/the-eus-ai-transparency-code-of-practice-explained/)
- Note: Article 50(4) also covers AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest. That is relevant to AI news explainers, but I did not verify the editorial-responsibility exemption details in these sources.

**US**
- California AI Transparency Act (SB 942 as amended by AB 853, signed Oct 13, 2025): operative Aug 2, 2026, moved from Jan 1, 2026. It applies to GenAI providers with more than 1M monthly users and requires latent and manifest disclosures plus a free detection tool. Penalty is $5,000 per violation, enforced by the AG or local authorities, with no private right of action. GenAI hosting-platform duties start Jan 1, 2027; capture-device duties apply from 2028. — [Orrick](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2025-10-17/california-delays-its-ai-transparency-act-and-passes-new-content-laws/); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/californias-ongoing-ai-regulation-key-deadlines-arriving-2026-and-beyond)
- New York GBL § 396-b (synthetic performers in ads): effective June 9, 2026. Advertisers must conspicuously disclose an AI-generated "synthetic performer" in an ad when they have actual knowledge. Penalties are $1,000 for the first violation and $5,000 for later ones. "Conspicuously" is undefined. — [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/new-york-synthetic-performer-law-what-advertisers-need-to-know); [McDermott](https://mcdermottlaw.com/insights/new-yorks-synthetic-performer-disclosure-law-what-advertisers-need-to-know)
- FTC: the final rule banning fake and AI-generated consumer reviews, testimonials and celebrity testimonials took effect Oct 21, 2024. The 2023 Endorsement Guides still govern sponsorship disclosure. — [Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2024/08/30/u-s-ftcs-new-rule-on-fake-and-ai-generated-reviews-and-social-media-bots/)
- A December 2025 federal executive order aims to curb state AI laws. Its effect on these state laws was not verified. — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/new-york-mandates-ai-disclosure-in-ads-as-white-house-moves-to-block-state-ai-laws/)

### Inferences
- A pipeline using C2PA-signing generators (many major video and image models embed it) should expect automatic, sometimes non-removable AI labels on YouTube, TikTok and Meta. Stripping metadata to avoid labels is risky: TikTok's invisible watermarks and classifiers are designed to catch it. For EU audiences, removing provider watermarks conflicts with deployer duties under the Code of Practice.
- For an operation that is not a GenAI provider, the main legal exposure is deployer-side: deepfakes or realistic depictions of real people and events need labels in the EU, and sponsored or ad content needs disclosure (NY synthetic performers, FTC endorsements). California SB 942 mainly binds GenAI providers, and hosting platforms from 2027.

### Gaps
- No primary Meta policy page retrieved confirming whether organic Reels creators must self-disclose photorealistic AI, beyond auto-labels.
- No controlled study found on whether AI labels reduce views or engagement on any platform.
- Effect of the Dec 2025 federal executive order on state laws is not verified.

## 3. TikTok Creator Rewards, originality, US joint venture; Instagram/Facebook originality ranking

### Takeaway
TikTok Creator Rewards pays only for original videos over one minute, with an opaque formula weighting originality, play duration, search value and engagement. Reported 2026 rates are roughly $0.40–$1.00 per 1,000 qualified views, from unofficial sources. US TikTok has been operated by the TikTok USDS Joint Venture since Jan 22, 2026, and creators report volatile RPM since then, but algorithm retraining effects are unconfirmed. Instagram (April 2026) and Facebook (2025, tightened March 2026) both stop recommending accounts that mostly repost unoriginal content.

### Cited Findings
**TikTok Creator Rewards**
- Commonly cited requirements: 18+, 10,000 followers, 100,000 views in the last 30 days, a personal account in an eligible country, and videos over one minute. Videos under 60s earn nothing from the program. — [ContentStudio](https://contentstudio.io/blog/tiktok-creativity-program); [Neal Schaffer](https://nealschaffer.com/tiktok-creator-rewards-program/)
- Qualified views are reported as For You views of 5s or more, or search views of 30s or more, on original videos over one minute, with a 1,000-qualified-view minimum per video. Duets, stitches, reposts and compilations are excluded. — [TokConnect (secondary)](https://tokconnect.com/guides/tiktok-creator-rewards-program/)
- The formula weights four factors: originality ("unique perspective or creative thought process"), play duration, search value and audience engagement. — [RouteNote](https://routenote.com/blog/tiktok-launch-creator-rewards-program/); [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/phones/tiktok-launches-new-creator-rewards-program-to-pay-creators-who-make-trending-search-content)
- Reported 2026 pay is about $0.40–$1.00 per 1,000 qualified views (creator reports; TikTok publishes no rate). US terms were reportedly last updated July 20, 2026 and issued by TikTok USDS Joint Venture LLC, which sets eligibility "in its sole discretion." — [TokConnect](https://tokconnect.com/guides/tiktok-creator-rewards-program/); [Thothium](https://thothium.com/blog/tiktok-oracle-algorithm-rebuild-2026)
- Unverified: some vendor blogs claim fully AI-generated content is ineligible. I could not confirm this in TikTok's own terms. — [Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/what-tiktoks-ai-monetization-restrictions-signal-for-creator-income/); [FlowShorts](https://flowshorts.app/blog/tiktok-monetization-requirements)
- The official Creator Academy page could not be retrieved (JS-rendered). — attempted [TikTok Creator Academy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/creator-rewards-program)

**US TikTok ownership (2026)**
- TikTok USDS Joint Venture LLC formally took over US operations on Jan 22, 2026. Oracle, Silver Lake and MGX hold 15% each; ByteDance retains 19.9%. The JV is tasked with retraining and securing the recommendation algorithm on US data. The Jan 23 announcement used the future tense, and on May 29, 2026 Senator Markey wrote that the JV "failed to explain" what retraining means. — [Thothium](https://thothium.com/blog/tiktok-oracle-algorithm-rebuild-2026); [FastClips](https://fastclips.app/learn/tiktok-us-algorithm-joint-venture)
- Creators have reported sharp, unpredictable RPM and reach drops since January 2026. Outages also disrupted posting and view counts, so the causes are unverified. — [FastClips](https://fastclips.app/learn/tiktok-us-algorithm-joint-venture); [ShortGen](https://shortgen.io/blog/tiktok-algorithm-change-2026)
- Conflict: one guide says commercial operations, including payouts, stayed with ByteDance's TikTok entity. That contradicts the July 2026 Creator Rewards terms being issued by the JV. — [Storika](https://www.storika.ai/guides/tiktok-us-joint-venture-influencer-marketing) vs [Thothium](https://thothium.com/blog/tiktok-oracle-algorithm-rebuild-2026)

**Instagram**
- In April 2026, Adam Mosseri extended Instagram's Reels originality rule to photos and carousels. Accounts that primarily share content they didn't create or meaningfully transform are no longer recommended to non-followers, though they still reach existing followers. Mosseri's suggested alternatives: remix, add your own commentary or voiceover, use the native repost button, or collab with the original creator. — [PetaPixel, Apr 30 2026](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/)
- Cropping, resizing, watermarks or credit tags do not count as transformation. — [Sociallyin (secondary)](https://sociallyin.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-update-repost/)

**Facebook**
- July 2025: Meta took action against about 500K spam or fake-engagement accounts (reduced distribution, demoted comments, monetization bans) and removed about 10M impersonator profiles. Minor edits, such as watermarks or stitching clips, are not "meaningful enhancements." Duplicate videos are demoted so originals get reach. — [TechCrunch, Jul 14 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/following-youtube-meta-announces-crackdown-on-unoriginal-facebook-content)
- March 2026 update to Feed and Reels: accounts that repeatedly share unoriginal content get reduced distribution and may lose recommendation or monetization eligibility; appeals are available. — [Business Standard, Mar 16 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/meta-revises-facebook-rules-to-curb-copied-content-in-feed-reels-and-more-content-creator-original-creator-reward-126031600488_1.html) (page blocked to fetch; summarized from search snippet)

### Inferences
- TikTok's over-60s requirement pushes in the opposite direction from Shorts, where 20–35s is often cited for completion rate. A cross-posting pipeline likely needs platform-specific cuts: 60–90s versions for TikTok monetization, shorter versions for Shorts and Reels reach.
- On all three Meta, YouTube and TikTok surfaces, "originality" is judged at the account level. Aggregated or reposted clips with AI voiceover are the clearest targets.

### Gaps
- No primary TikTok document confirming the AI-content stance in Creator Rewards, or the exact qualified-view definition.
- Whether and when the US algorithm retraining actually shipped is unconfirmed.
- Facebook Reels and Snapchat Spotlight payout specifics for 2026 were not researched.

## 4. What performs in 2026: retention, length, hooks, formats, AI-slop fatigue, case studies (incl. skeptical views)

### Takeaway
Hard, independent performance data is scarce. Most retention and length "benchmarks" come from vendor blogs and conflict with each other. The best-documented facts are that AI slop does get massive reach (21% of Shorts served to new users) and that top slop channels earned millions. At the same time, audience trust in AI content is falling, platforms are adding slop-feedback and AI-filter controls, and Shorts RPM is tiny. Skeptical data suggests most automation channels never monetize.

### Cited Findings
**Retention, length, hooks (low confidence: vendor data)**
- Retention benchmarks conflict. One source gives Shorts 40–55%, TikTok 40–50% and Reels 45–65%; another lists TikTok 78%, Shorts 73% and Reels 65%. They likely measure different things. — [Retensis](https://retensis.com/blog/audience-retention-benchmarks-2026); [Increditors](https://increditors.com/video-retention-benchmarks/)
- Length "sweet spots" conflict. One source claims Shorts do best at 20–25s for completion, Reels at 60–90s for engagement, and TikTok at 21–34s; another finds 50–60s videos average the most views. No authoritative optimal length exists. — [Shno](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/short-form-video-statistics); [Vidico](https://vidico.com/news/short-form-video-statistics/)
- Wistia's 2026 State of Video reports videos under one minute at a 52% engagement rate. This is the more citable figure; the widely repeated "50% retention under 90 seconds" claim has no traceable source. — [Vidico](https://vidico.com/news/short-form-video-statistics/)
- Hook claims: "71% of viewers decide within the first few seconds," and a target of more than 80% retention in the first 3 seconds on Shorts. These are rules of thumb, not measured standards. — [Humble & Brag](https://humbleandbrag.com/blog/youtube-shorts-benchmarks)

**Formats**
- Reddit story narration over split-screen gameplay (e.g., Subway Surfers) is still promoted by tool vendors as "the most viral format," with an unsourced claimed completion rate above 75%. I found no independent evidence either way on whether the format has declined in 2026. — [GhostShorts (vendor)](https://ghostshorts.com/blog/how-to-make-split-screen-gaming-videos-2026)
- What dominated Kapwing's top slop channels: AI animal-in-human-scenario stories (Bandar Apna Dost), AI fairy-tale storytelling (Cuentos Facinantes), and wisdom or quote narration (Three Minutes Wisdom, monetized partly via Coupang affiliate links). — [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/blog/ai-slop-report-the-global-rise-of-low-quality-ai-videos/)
  - Note: several of these large channels were among those removed or wiped in January 2026. The articles don't name the removed channels, so I can't confirm which.

**Audience fatigue and trust**
- Gartner (June 2026): 49% of US consumers say GenAI has made content quality worse. — [CX Today](https://www.cxtoday.com/marketing-sales-technology/gartner-ai-generated-content-quality-marketing-fatigue/)

### Inferences
- Reach is not the bottleneck for AI slop; durability and monetization are. Channels that grow fastest on templated AI formats are the ones that match the policy's examples, so in this evidence base, high reach and high termination risk go together.
- Given Shorts RPM, the 2027 Shorts floor, and TikTok's over-60s requirement, the economics favor using shorts as top-of-funnel for higher-RPM long-form, affiliate, product or brand deals, and favor higher-CPM niches (finance, tech, education) over entertainment slop.

### Gaps
- No independent, platform-verified 2026 retention or length benchmarks. No analytics-firm (e.g., Tubular, Dash Hudson) data retrieved.
- No hard data on whether Reddit-story or split-screen formats are declining.
- No verified named case studies of mid-sized automated Shorts channels, with revenue, that survived 2026 enforcement.

## 5. Practices that keep automated content on the right side of policy

### Takeaway
The policy texts consistently reward variation, original narrative or commentary, and transformation, and penalize templates, readings of others' material, and unmodified compilations. Disclosure is necessary but not sufficient. No platform publishes a posting-cadence limit, but sameness across the catalog is what triggers channel-level enforcement.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube explicitly allows AI used to visualize "a unique character and narrative you invented," AI-edited scripts, and unique AI backgrounds. It disallows generic or unoriginal templates, same-outcome storylines, and slideshows or scrolling text without narrative, commentary or educational value. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Reused material must show a "meaningful difference" from the original; readings of text you didn't create are not allowed. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- YouTube's disclosure page treats AI scripting, captions, thumbnails and self-voice-cloning as production assistance that needs no label. Realistic synthetic scenes and real-person depictions need a label. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- Channels that survive reportedly add a human layer: a hosted point of view, original research, or distinctive editing. Some are adding on-camera hosts. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026/); [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)
- Instagram: remix, add commentary or voiceover, use native repost or collab, rather than reposting. Watermarks and credit tags are not enough. — [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/)
- Facebook: watermarks or stitching are not meaningful enhancements; originals win distribution over duplicates. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/following-youtube-meta-announces-crackdown-on-unoriginal-facebook-content)
- TikTok rewards originality and "unique perspective" as a payout factor and excludes reposts and compilations. — [RouteNote](https://routenote.com/blog/tiktok-launch-creator-rewards-program/); [TokConnect](https://tokconnect.com/guides/tiktok-creator-rewards-program/)
- Don't strip AI watermarks or metadata. EU deployers must not remove provider markings, and TikTok's invisible watermarks are built to survive re-uploads. — [Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content); [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/tiktok-ssa-shares-more-ways-to-spot-shape-and-understand-ai-generated-content?lang=en-ZA)

### Inferences
- Operational guardrails that map to the written policy:
  1. Original scripts, with no readings of scraped Reddit or news text.
  2. Varied structure, visuals and outcomes across uploads; avoid a single repeated template.
  3. A consistent human editorial voice or persona, which can be AI-assisted but carries a real point of view.
  4. Fewer, better uploads per channel.
  5. Keep C2PA intact and toggle disclosure for any realistic synthetic scene.
  6. Audit the catalog as a whole, because enforcement is channel-level.
- A "human in the loop" step that only approves outputs probably doesn't change how templated the catalog looks. The test platforms state is what the viewer sees: narrative, commentary, educational value, or a meaningful difference.

### Gaps
- No platform publishes a safe posting frequency. I found no credible data linking cadence to enforcement.
- No official guidance quantifies how much human input is enough.
